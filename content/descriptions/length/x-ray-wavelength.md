When an electron drops into an inner shell of a copper atom, the emitted X-ray can have a wavelength of 0.15405925 nm. This particular line matters because X-ray wavelengths are comparable with the spacing of atoms in a crystal, allowing diffraction patterns to reveal atomic structure. The number is a measured **line wavelength**, not a boundary between X-rays and other radiation. The copper K-alpha spectrum contains nearby components too; [NIST measured their distinct wavelengths](https://tsapps.nist.gov/publication/get_pdf.cfm?pub_id=920358).
