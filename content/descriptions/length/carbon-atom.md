The 140 pm marker is twice carbon's **empirical atomic radius** of 70 pm, a useful comparison number rather than an edge you could touch. Carbon's nonbonded, van der Waals radius is about 170 pm, so the two conventions answer different questions.

In the explorer, dots sample where carbon's six electrons are likely to be and stop at the 70 pm sphere, which holds a little over half of their probability. The tight blue core is the 1s pair; the amber cloud is the 2s pair; and the green and rose dumbbells are the two occupied 2p orbitals, which point at right angles to each other and give carbon its directional bonds. The orbitals are hydrogen-like approximations with screened nuclear charges, not an exact six-electron solution. [WebElements lists both radii](https://webelements.co.uk/carbon/atom_sizes.html).
