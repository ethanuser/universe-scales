Water's bend matters more than its rough diameter. Its two O–H bonds meet at about 104.5°, so their electrical polarities reinforce rather than cancel; neighboring molecules can then form a shifting network of hydrogen bonds. The 2.75 Å marker is an approximate overall molecular diameter, not a bond length. In an isolated molecule, each O–H distance is only 0.958 Å, as recorded in [NIST's experimental geometry](https://cccbdb.nist.gov/expgeom2x.asp?casno=7732185).
