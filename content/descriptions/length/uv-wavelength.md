A low-pressure mercury lamp emits much of its radiation at 253.7 nm, in the ultraviolet. Germicidal lamps use that line to damage microbial genetic material in air and on surfaces. The wavelength is shorter than visible violet light, so the useful radiation itself cannot be judged by eye. The marker shows a particular lamp emission line, not the whole ultraviolet band; exposure depends on lamp design and placement. [CDC describes its wavelength and use](https://www.cdc.gov/infection-control/hcp/environmental-control/air.html).
