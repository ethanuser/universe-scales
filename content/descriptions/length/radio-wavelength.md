At 100 MHz, a radio wave travels about three meters during one oscillation: $\lambda=c/f$. This is a familiar point inside the US FM broadcast band, which extends from 88 to 108 MHz. An antenna need not be three meters long; designs often use fractions of the wavelength and depend on surrounding conductors. The marker represents one example frequency, while radio wavelengths span many orders of magnitude. [The FM band is specified in federal regulations](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-47/chapter-I/subchapter-C/part-73/subpart-H/section-73.201).
