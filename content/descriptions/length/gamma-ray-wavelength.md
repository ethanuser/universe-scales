After cobalt-60 decays, its daughter nickel-60 releases a gamma ray with about 1.173 MeV of energy. The corresponding wavelength is only about 1.06 pm, calculated from $\lambda=hc/E$. That is much shorter than an atom's diameter. This marker represents **one spectral line**, not all gamma rays. The paired 1.332 MeV line from the same decay would sit at a slightly shorter wavelength. [NIST describes the cobalt-60 gamma cascade](https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/jres/088/3/V88-3.pdf).
